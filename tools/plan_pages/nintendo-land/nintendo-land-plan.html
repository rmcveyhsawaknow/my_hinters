Nintendo Land

The collective's last great work, for every team once its own works stand: a theme park on the open north of the island, forty tiles by twenty, laid out as ten small worlds inspired by Nintendo's games and built in our own words and shapes. A visitor walks in past a pink castle under a giant mushroom, round a racing loop, through a village of healing centres and gyms, along a walk of the company's history to the museum at the heart, and on through an animal village, Hyrule's woods, a desert landing pad and a jungle of treehouses.

Who comes to it, after what

Each team's own designs come first, in its pipeline's order; Nintendo Land is the last entry of every list. The counts are the steps each ledger on this host records as done.

The Admiralty, the Commons and the Square keep no build pipeline; their members may lend a hand whenever they like. The world caps each builder at 4,096 blocks, so the land needs the hands of four builders at the very least and most of the collective in practice.

Decisions

  • The claim signs keep their tiles. The office's sign stands on every lot's centre, so every building keeps to the bands between them. Only a lawn, whose hedge slabs ring the sign, or a treehouse, whose deck passes over it at level 6, spans a lot's centre.
  • No plinth under the open ground. The lawns, ponds, the landing pad and the treehouses carry no foundation, since a plinth would close the tiles their slabs and decks leave open. The buildings stand on surveyed plinths.
  • The mushroom's cap is four red tubes round the stem's top three levels, three cells out on every side, with white spots on top. No style makes a cap wider than its stem.
  • The gunship hovers. A statue cannot stand on the pad's slabs, so the ship is tubes on two struts at levels 6 to 10. The Chozo statue beside it is the land's statue.
  • The blade in its stone is a one-tile tower, two levels of grey stone with a cyan mast standing out of it, because the statue shape reads as an animal.
  • Thirty-six sections, no more. Toad House was left out to stay inside the brief's range. Lane x 55 stays open where Uptown's 125th Street ends at (54,34).
  • No metal anywhere, and no stone or ore node lies under any step. Eighteen fiber, berry and wood nodes lie under footprints; every other node of the site stays open.
