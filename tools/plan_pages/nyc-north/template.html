New York, the northern replica

North of the finished Lower Manhattan, the replica goes up in three more designs. Midtown's skyline comes first, then Central Park round the inland lake, then Uptown north of the park. Every building and monument has a sign beside it, and every step rises on ground the land office sells to the Atelier one segment at a time.

Section schedule

One row per building or monument, in building order. Tiles are inclusive (x0,y0 to x1,y1). Top is the highest level a block reaches, from 0 to 15, plinth included. Each sign is the section's last step, on the tile named.

The land, segment by segment

The land office offers each segment to Plumbline once the one before it is deeded. If a price is refused, the office offers it again two hours later. Plumbline's steps rise only on lots whose deed names it. The office's own hands may build on their lots before the deed is paid, and the deed then carries the ground with whatever stands on it.

Totals and materials

Items are what the steps cost: one item per four blocks, and one wood for each sign. Stone pays for stone blocks, wood for wood blocks and fiber for thatch. No design uses metal, so no ore is needed. Every box is at most 160 cells, so a pack of fifty with its berries set aside pays for any single step.

How progress is read

    Decisions in Uptown

    • The wrap unfolded. In the real city, Uptown wraps around the park, with the Upper West Side on its west, the Upper East Side on its east and Harlem to the north. The glass has ground only to the north. So the West Side's landmarks stand on the west lots, Harlem's in the middle and the East Side's on the east lots, and each building keeps its real side of Broadway and Fifth Avenue.
    • The real order, where the nodes allow. West of Broadway, the order from north to south is the city's own: the Cloisters, Grant's Tomb, Riverside Church, then the Natural History Museum. Low Library stands north of 125th Street because that is where the forest leaves a clear three tiles by two. St John the Divine takes the one clear square south of it.
    • No Dakota and no Lincoln Center. The wood, berry and fiber nodes on the west lots leave room for only eight sections there.
    • Nodes stay open. No building covers a node. Morningside Park's lawn lies over the valley's berry and fiber nodes, and the four streets cross nine nodes. Lawns and streets are slabs that close no tile, so walkers and gatherers can still reach every node.
    • The elevated station is the Park Avenue one. Harlem's commuter station sits on 125th Street east of Fifth Avenue, under a viaduct at level 6 that rests on the station's roof and two piers. Broadway has a street on every tile, which leaves no room for piers.
    • Heights. Riverside Church's tower reaches level 13, the same as St Patrick's. Only Midtown's Empire State Building reaches the ceiling.
